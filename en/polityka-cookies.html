1. What are cookies?

Cookies are small text files stored on the Client's device (computer, smartphone) when visiting the Website. They are used to improve how the site works, personalise content and analyse traffic.

2. Types of cookies used on our site

  • Necessary cookies – enable the site to work properly: remembering your choice regarding cookies and language, logging in to the client account and the operation of the booking form. They do not require consent and cannot be switched off.
  • Analytics cookies – Google Analytics 4 (files _ga, _ga_*): counting visits and analysing how the site is used. They require consent.
  • Advertising cookies – Google Ads cookies (_gcl_*) recording an ad click on your device, and our record of the source of a visit from an ad (click identifier and campaign parameters, vc_first_touch, stored for up to 90 days), used solely to attribute a booking to a campaign. They require consent.
  • Ad measurement – passing data about a visit, enquiry or phone call to Google in order to measure the effectiveness of ads. Requires consent.
  • Ad personalisation – matching ads to your interests (Google remarketing lists, Meta audiences). Requires consent. Meta Pixel (file _fbp) is launched only when consent is given to all three advertising categories.

3. Managing cookies

You can check, change or withdraw your preferences at any time in the Cookie settings section below. A "Cookie settings" link is also in the footer of every page. In addition, most browsers allow you to block or delete cookies.

4. Cookie retention period

We store your cookie choice for 12 months, after which we will ask again. Analytics and advertising cookies are stored in line with the policies of the tool providers (Google, Meta), no longer than your consent applies.

5. Consent

We store optional cookies only after you have given your consent. No choice, scrolling the page or continuing to use it does not mean consent — in that case only necessary cookies operate. You can withdraw consent at any time, as easily as it was given; withdrawal of consent does not affect the lawfulness of actions taken before it was withdrawn.
